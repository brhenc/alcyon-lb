## 0. Short answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is HTTP/3 "largely functional"? | **Yes, for what a request needs.** 26 of 26 rows of the `curl --http3-only` matrix (GET, POST 3 B / 1 MiB / 1 MiB chunked, 10 and 100 MiB downloads, chunked response, 304/404/500, `http-request return`/`deny`, `use_backend`, 5 keep-alive requests on one connection, 20 parallel streams, alt-svc, 2 s slow backend, 8 KB header, HEAD, `TE: trailers`) give the **same status, size and body as HAProxy**, the log line is `"GET https://host/ HTTP/3.0"` on both, `h2load --alpn-list=h3` 20/20 on both, graceful stop (SIGUSR1) completes the in-flight request and refuses new connections on both, SIGTERM behaves the same (4.1-4.3). |
| Then what is missing? | The things around the request: (1) the **standard deployment config does not work** — a TCP `ssl` bind and a `quic4@` bind on the same ip:port (how every HAProxy h3 site is written, alt-svc `h3=":443"`) gives the QUIC listener the TCP bind's TLS config and curl fails in 2 ms (5.1); (2) **200 simultaneous connections**: 2-91 of 200 time out on Alcyon, 0 of 200 on HAProxy, three runs each at nbthread 1/4/16 (5.2); (3) a **reload** loses about 1 request in 600 (`ngtcp2 ERR_DRAINING`), HAProxy 0 in 245 (5.3); (4) QUIC connections are **invisible to `maxconn`, `scur`, `CurrConns`, `CurrSslConns`, `CumSslConns`**, and `stot`/`conn_tot` count request streams, not connections (5.4); (5) every `tune.quic.*`, `quic-cc-algo`, `quic-socket`, `quic-initial`, `allow-0rtt`, `quic-force-retry` is **refused** (correct per AGENTS.md §5, but an operator cannot set an idle timeout or a stream limit), `-cc feature(QUIC)` answers false and the **33 QUIC reg-tests all SKIP** — and could not pass anyway, because every one of them uses HAProxy itself as the HTTP/3 client (`server ... quic4@`, `alpn h3`, `proto qmux`), which Alcyon refuses and rustls 0.24.0-dev.2 cannot provide (`FromConn<ClientSide>` is `todo!`, T461) (4.4); (6) no browser has been seen on Alcyon over h3 yet (headless Chrome fails against the oracle as well today, so this is tooling, not Alcyon, 4.5). |
| Architecture to know about | An HTTP/3 request stream reaches the engine as **an HTTP/1 message through a `socketpair`** (`quic_listener.rs::start_relay`, `h3_relay.rs`): 2 fds and two kernel copies per in-flight request (measured: +60 fds for 20 in-flight requests on Alcyon, +21 on HAProxy, 4.3), response trailers dropped, interim (1xx) responses dropped. It is correct and it is what made T130 possible in a day; it is not what HAProxy does (`mux_quic.c`/`h3.c` go to HTX in process). M2 does **not** require replacing it (section 1 and slice 11): measure first. |
| How far from M2? | **13 slices: 3 S, 9 M, 1 L-conditional** (section 6). Slices 1-6 make h3 safe for a blog (the two config/burst defects, accounting under `maxconn`, reload loss, T492, T493); 7-10 are parity and verification (knobs, `show quic`, reg-tests, topologies); 11-13 are performance and documentation. At this project's cadence (one M slice ≈ one agent session with its gate) that is roughly **two working weeks**, plus the night measurements and a 24 h soak that only the user's schedule can place. |

## 1. Scope (in)

The goal is the project's: a small deployment (a blog) behind Alcyon can turn on HTTP/3 the way it
would on HAProxy, with no crash, leak, deadlock or hang, and performance roughly comparable.

1. **The listener as HAProxy's.** `bind quic4@` / `quic6@` with `ssl crt ... alpn h3`, on the
   **same ip:port as a TCP `ssl` bind** of the same frontend; `-c` and `-cc feature(QUIC)`;
   `nbthread` ≥ 1 (T210's prebind stays); `alt-svc` by `http-response set-header` (as HAProxy:
   there is no automatic advertisement in either).
2. **HTTP/3 to clients, HTTP/1 and h2 to servers.** Request and response semantics equal to
   HAProxy's on the matrix of 4.1 and on HAProxy's own h3 reg-tests where they can run (4.4):
   methods, bodies with and without `content-length`, chunked both ways, status codes incl. 1xx
   and 304, `http-request`/`http-response` rules, `use_backend`, load balancing, persistence,
   `option forwardfor`, the log line (`%HV` = `HTTP/3.0`, `fe~`), `show stat` and `show info`
   counters **per QUIC connection** as HAProxy counts them.
3. **Operations.** `maxconn` (global and frontend) bounds QUIC connections; `timeout client`,
   `timeout http-request`, `timeout http-keep-alive` apply to h3; seamless reload (SIGUSR2 / `-x`
   `-sf`) with **zero** lost h3 requests over 100 reloads; graceful stop (SIGUSR1) drains h3 as
   today; `hard-stop-after` bounds it (T108); `show quic` with HAProxy's default columns; the
   `tune.quic.*` an operator reaches for — `tune.quic.fe.max-idle-timeout`,
   `tune.quic.fe.stream.max-concurrent`, `tune.quic.fe.sec.retry-threshold`, `tune.quic.listen` —
   reach the data path; the other 27 stay refused with a message naming the keyword.
4. **Robustness (the R1 items applied to QUIC).** The red team's harness
   (`review/quic-review-2026-10-25/B-harness/`, ~70 cases) and netfuzz's QUIC slice run one
   full cycle on the candidate with no medium finding; T492 (Retry / half-open cap) and T493
   (per-connection costs and retention) closed; 200 simultaneous handshakes complete; 24 h soak
   with mixed h1/h2/h3 traffic, RSS flat.
5. **Practical topologies over h3** (scripts under `tools/practical/`, run against both proxies,
   same PASS/FAIL table, as M1): **07-h3** — the matrix of 4.1 as a script; **01-node over h3** —
   keep-alive, 100 MB chunked upload, 100 MB download, SSE 10 min, slow response, backend 5xx (no
   WebSocket: RFC 9220 is out, below); **06-browsers over h3** — Chrome and Firefox (headless)
   reach the node SPA over TLS/h2, read `alt-svc`, come back over h3 (checked in the proxy log by
   `HTTP/3.0`), no developer-tools error.
6. **Reg-tests.** The 33 `feature(QUIC)` tests of `reg-tests/quic/`, `qmux/` and
   `http-messaging/h3_trailers.vtc` are **run**, not skipped: the harness starts the vtc's
   client-side `haproxy` blocks (the ones with `server ... quic4@`/`alpn h3`/`proto qmux`) on the
   oracle binary and the server-side blocks on Alcyon; a test that can only be served by a QUIC
   *client* in Alcyon is `OUT_OF_SCOPE` with that reason, not `SKIP`.

## 2. Refused (the parser says so, by keyword)

Measured today: every item below is already refused with an explicit message (4.4); M2 keeps it so.

- **QUIC to servers** (`server ... quic4@`, `proto qmux`, `ssl alpn h3` to a QUIC listener,
  `ssl-f-use`'s QUIC side): rustls 0.24 has no QUIC client; HAProxy itself still calls it
  experimental (`expose-experimental-directives`). Revisit when rustls 0.24.0 ships one.
- **0-RTT** (`allow-0rtt` on a `quic` bind): off (`max_early_data_size 0`, red-team case); the
  replay surface is not worth a blog's first-request RTT.
- **WebSocket over h3** (RFC 9220 extended CONNECT), **WebTransport**, **QUIC datagrams**, **MASQUE**,
  **server push** (`SETTINGS_ENABLE_PUSH`): HAProxy has none of them either.
- **QPACK dynamic table**: Alcyon advertises capacity 0 as HAProxy does (`quic_parity.md` §2); stays.
- `quic-cc-algo` other than cubic, `quic-socket`, `quic-initial` rules, `tune.quic.*` outside the
  four of section 1.3, `tune.quic.zero-copy-fwd-send`, `quic-force-retry` (Retry itself arrives
  with T492; the forcing keyword is a test aid, refused until a test needs it).
- A QUIC listener on a `mode tcp` frontend (HAProxy refuses it: `MUX protocol 'quic' is not
  usable for 'bind quic4@…'`; Alcyon accepts it today, slice 1 makes it refuse).

### 4.6 Robustness evidence (the red team's, cited, not repeated)

`~/ibex-worktrees/review/quic-review-2026-10-25.md`: no Alcyon crash, hang or memory growth in ~70
wire cases vs the same oracle (malformed CRYPTO, transport parameters, key update, 0-RTT, CID
limits, PATH_CHALLENGE, junk streams, idle); Alcyon stricter and RFC-correct where it differs; the
crypto port matches quinn-proto item by item. Defects filed: **T492** (Retry can never be enabled,
no half-open cap, `enable_stateless_reset` never read; M) and **T493** (`update_next_timeout`
walks all connections per packet, `answered` Vec never pruned, ≈230 KB RSS kept per connection
after idle close, to check at scale; M). Suspected and unmeasured there: one bad peer address

## 5. Findings with reproducers (not filed as tasks; slices 1, 3, 6, 2 own them)

### 5.1 TCP `ssl` bind and `quic4@` bind on the same ip:port: h3 fails (S)

```
frontend fe
    bind 127.0.0.1:61040 ssl crt examples/certs/localhost.pem alpn h2,http/1.1
    bind quic4@127.0.0.1:61040 ssl crt examples/certs/localhost.pem alpn h3
    default_backend be
$ curl -sk --http3-only --resolve localhost:61040:127.0.0.1 -o /dev/null -w '%{http_version} %{http_code}\n' https://localhost:61040/
0 000          # HAProxy: 3 200.  curl -v: "QUIC connect to 127.0.0.1 port 61040 failed: Could not connect to server" after 2 ms
```
Bisect (each a fresh `alcyon -f`): QUIC bind alone `3 200`; TCP bind on another port `3 200`; QUIC
line **before** the TCP line `3 200`; TCP bind **without** `ssl` on the same port `3 200`; TCP
`ssl` bind without `alpn` on the same port `0 000`. Cause: `engine.rs::bind_tls_with`
(`binds.iter().find(|b| b.addr == addr && b.ssl)`) is shared by `bind_tls` and `bind_tls_quic`,
so the QUIC listener is built from the first `ssl` bind at that address — the TCP one, with ALPN
`h2,http/1.1` and TLS 1.2 allowed — and the client's `h3` ALPN is refused. Fix: `bind_tls_quic`
filters on `b.quic` (and `bind_tls` on `!b.quic`). Test first: `h3_proxy_path`-style, two binds
on one reserved port, TCP line first, h3 request `200`; also the h2 request on the TCP bind still
negotiates `h2` (today it might take the QUIC bind's TLS when the QUIC line is first).

### 5.2 200 simultaneous h3 connections: some never complete (M)

```
$ python3 ~/ibex-worktrees/review/m2-plan-2026-10-25/idle_h3.py <quic port> 200 1      # aioquic 1.3.0
HAProxy:   held ok=200 fail=0 errs={}        (twice)
Alcyon, fresh instance, bind quic4@ only, backend = be.py:
  nbthread 1  : ok=153 fail=47 | ok=183 fail=17 | ok=143 fail=57
  nbthread 4  : ok=109 fail=91 | ok=200 fail=0  | ok=185 fail=15
  nbthread 16 : ok=170 fail=30 | ok=189 fail=11 | ok=191 fail=9        all errs={'TimeoutError': n} (10 s)
  50 at once: fail=0.  10 batches of 20 (sequential): fail=0.
```
Not thread-count dependent, so not T210's reuseport hashing; the connection either never
finishes its handshake or its single request is never answered within 10 s. Candidates, to be
measured not assumed: quinn-proto's handshake timers not re-armed when many `Incoming` arrive in
one `recv_batch` (`BATCH_SIZE`), the per-datagram `update_next_timeout` walk (T493 #1) under a
burst, the anti-amplification budget starving retransmissions, or a lost `Initial` beyond the
recv buffer (`DgramSocket` reports truncation; a full socket buffer drops silently — compare
`net.core.rmem_default` handling with HAProxy's `quic_sock` buffers). Diagnose with the harness
(per-connection timeline: Initial sent, Handshake received, request sent, response) and an Alcyon
counter for datagrams received vs connections created.

### 5.3 A reload loses an h3 request now and then (M)

```
$ ~/ibex-worktrees/review/m2-plan-2026-10-25/reload-alcyon2.sh      # 12 s of back-to-back curl --http3-only GET /, SIGUSR2 to $(cat alcyon.pid) at 2 s, 5 s, 8 s
codes: 585 × 200, 1 × 000, 1 × ERR ; curl: (56) ngtcp2_conn_writev_stream returned error: ERR_DRAINING
(earlier run: 746 × 200, 1 × 000, 1 × ERR; another: 643 × 200)             HAProxy, same script: 245 × 200
```
`ERR_DRAINING` means the client received `CONNECTION_CLOSE` while it still had a request to
send on that connection: the old process closed a connection whose next request was in flight.
T108 made the old process GOAWAY and close "once its responses are acknowledged"; a request
that arrives after the GOAWAY but before the close is the gap (HAProxy keeps the connection until
the client closes or `timeout client` fires, then closes). The `000` is probably the same window
seen from a connection that had not finished its handshake. Measure HAProxy's exact drain
sequence (GOAWAY stream id, when CONNECTION_CLOSE is sent) with the red team's harness first.
Note for the operator rows: Alcyon's `-W` process re-execs itself on SIGUSR2 (new PID in the
pidfile, `took over 4 listening socket(s)`), HAProxy's master keeps its PID; my first run signalled
the draining old PID twice, harmlessly.

### 5.4 QUIC connections are outside `maxconn` and the connection counters (M)

```
frontend fe
    maxconn 2
    bind quic4@127.0.0.1:<port> ssl crt … alpn h3
$ python3 idle_h3.py <port> 6 3 &; echo 'show stat' | socat - UNIX-CONNECT:<sock> | grep ^fe,FRONTEND | cut -d, -f5,7
HAProxy: 2,2   (4 connections wait)      Alcyon: 0,2   (all 6 served: "held ok=6 fail=0")
```
With 200 idle connections `show info` says `CurrConns: 200 CurrSslConns: 200` on HAProxy and
`CurrConns: 0 CurrSslConns: 0` on Alcyon; `fe stot` counts streams (`6`) where HAProxy counts
connections (`2`). `quic_listener.rs:428` ticks `count_global_conn/sess` once per handshake
(rates only); `engine.rs:5843` gives an h3 stream "no listener slot of its own". For a blog this
is the difference between `maxconn` protecting the box and not.
